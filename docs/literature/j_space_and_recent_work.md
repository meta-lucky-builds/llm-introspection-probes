## Part 1. J-space

### 1.1 What the J-lens is [read-excerpts, 2607.15495 sections 2.1-2.5, A.7, A.8]

Source: Gurnee, Sofroniew, ... Batson, Lindsey (Anthropic), "Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models", arXiv 2607.15495v1 (16 Jul 2026); HTML at transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/.

- Per layer l: `J_l = E over (source position t, target positions t' >= t, prompts) of d h_final,t' / d h_l,t`. One d_model x d_model matrix per layer. Expectation over 1,000 pretraining-like prompts.
- Readout: `lens(h_l) = softmax(W_U norm(J_l h_l))`. J_l = I gives the logit lens. Rows of `W_U J_l` are the "J-lens vectors", one direction per vocabulary token. They are overcomplete (n_vocab > d_model) and non-orthogonal.
- J-space (their definition): points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of at most k J-lens vectors (k typically <= 25; they report that about 25 is the number meaningfully active at once). The J-space component of an activation is found by gradient pursuit; the rest is the non-J-space component. The J-space component is never more than 10% of activation variance (varies by layer).
- Reading modes: ranked top tokens; per-token probe (inner product with one J-lens vector); sparse decomposition for a discrete inventory.
- Writing modes: steering `h <- h + alpha v_t`; ablation (project out); "patching in lens coordinates" which swaps the coefficients of two J-lens vectors and leaves the orthogonal complement untouched.
- Implementation detail that differs from the headline formula: appendix A.7 says the default lens on Sonnet 4.5 takes the target z at the penultimate layer, not the final block, because including the last block adds artifacts. Other implementations (J++ repo) use the final block. Treat the target layer as a free implementation choice.
- Cost. Paper's pseudocode (A.7): one backward pass per output dimension (d_model passes per prompt, batched), accumulating the Jacobian for every layer at once, then average. The paper text I saw gives no wall-clock or GPU-hour figure. The reference repo README [README] says fitting time is dominated by the model's own backward pass, that quality saturates quickly, and that about 100 prompts is usable (vs 1,000 x 128 tokens in the paper). J-CoT (2607.21981) used randomized low-rank estimates on 1,000 sequences of 128 tokens. Applying a fitted lens is one matrix multiply per layer; the paper says so in section 9.2.
- Paper's own stated limits [read-excerpts, section 9.1-9.3, 1.3]: only single-token concepts (multi-token via "template lens" and "oracle lens" in A.9, which the authors call proof of concept and tuned-lens-like); a bag of concepts with no binding; roughly the first third of layers gives noisy, largely uninterpretable readouts; no recurrence and no clear input processors; unclear whether competition for access resembles brain "ignition".

### 1.2 The five properties and evidence [mostly web-summary]

The abstract and section 1.3 [read-excerpts] name: verbal report, directed modulation, internal reasoning, flexible generalization, selectivity; plus structural signatures: coherent content only in an intermediate layer band, capacity of tens of concepts, broader weight-level broadcast.

Numbers below came from an LLM summary of the HTML page [web-summary] and were NOT checked against the arXiv text. Use as pointers only.
- Verbal report: swapping the J-space component of a concept vector reaches top-5 on 59% of trials vs 88% for pure J-lens vectors and 5% for the non-J-space component (summary). In the arXiv text [read-excerpts, section 3.1] the swap experiment is described across 14 categories and "reliably shifts the implanted concept toward the top of the output distribution"; no percentage seen by me.
- Internal reasoning: two-hop coordinate-swap success 54% (Haiku 4.5), 70% (Sonnet 4.5), 70% (Opus 4.5) (summary).
- Flexible generalization: France to China swap, 76/192, 101/192 at alpha = 2 (summary).
- Selectivity: ablating J-space leaves MMLU, SQuAD, sentiment near baseline, multi-hop reasoning falls "near zero" under heavy ablation (summary). Small samples reported (n=8, n=11 in some experiments, summary).
- Directed modulation: rate "tends to increase with model size", no figures visible (summary).

INFERENCE on strength: the causal swap and ablation experiments are interventional, which is stronger than probe correlations. But (i) all primary experiments are on Claude models (Sonnet 4.5 default; Haiku 4.5, Opus 4.5, some Opus 4.6) that outsiders cannot run; (ii) the global-workspace framing is a functional analogy the authors hedge themselves; (iii) swap failures concentrate where the source concept's lens vector was not active (section 9.1), so the effect is conditional on the lens covering the concept.

### 1.3 Introspection-relevant detail [read-excerpts, section 3.1]

The authors adapt Lindsey's injected-thought protocol. With the model told a thought may have been implanted and prefilled with a detection claim, the most likely next token was "elephant", and "elephant" appeared as a top J-lens token at a position in the user prompt (the comma after "If so"). They hypothesised the model attends to J-space at user-prompt positions when answering. Re-sampling while injecting a single J-lens vector on the user turn made the model "report the injected concept in the majority of trials"; the injected vector did not make the model say the word at earlier assistant positions, i.e. the effect is specific to the response position. No numbers seen by me. This is concept identity under a prompt that asks about injection. I did not see any test of which position was injected, or of prompts that do not mention injection.

### 1.5 October 2026 Circuits Update [web-summary]

transformer-circuits.pub/2026/october-update/: one item, "The J++ Lens" (Anthropic Fellows project led by Kola Ayonrinde, co-author Jack Lindsey). Per the page summary: per-layer k-means clusters of activations (E = 8 per the repo README), one expert Jacobian per cluster combined with learned signed weights, plus a layerwise-relevance-propagation backward pass (from R-Lens) and token filtering at readout. Still one linear map per layer. Qualitative claim only on the page. The repo README [README, read] reports on Qwen3.6-27B: 55.2% recall@10 on five latent-variable tasks vs 35.7% for the J-lens and 37.7% for the R-Lens, scored on items the expert weights did not see. Code: github.com/safety-research/jpp_lens (Apache-2.0; needs about 54 GB for Qwen3.6-27B in bf16). The update does not discuss introspection or monitoring (summary).

### 1.6 Theory

- 2610.31955, Ostrow, Qian, Liu, Fiete (MIT, Cornell), "A Mathematical Theory of the Jacobian Lens" [read-excerpts]. Claim: the mean Jacobian preferentially picks directions that are large in magnitude across depth, lag-coherent across token positions, and context-coherent, "coherent features" in a Lyapunov-mode sense. Validated on a Bayes filter and a two-layer toy transformer (residual dim 128) trained on HMM data; stated limitation: toy models only. Notes that individual and mean Jacobians differ strongly in four open models (gemma-2-2b, Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3-14B; PCA of singular values). Explicitly agnostic about whether this is a "Global Workspace". INFERENCE: this predicts the lens favours persistent, context-stable content (topic-like, instructed-to-hold content) and can underweight transient, context-specific content, such as a one-off injected token at one position.
- 2608.25347, Yan, Ding, ... Ling (Alibaba), "Short Horizons and Sparse Concepts" [read-excerpts]. Claim: J-lens is an averaged first-order transfer operator; under Gaussian inputs it equals the least-squares linear readout (Stein); non-Gaussian and nonlinear bias grow with distance from the output, explaining early-layer failure; Jacobian energy is concentrated in a few position pairs, diagonal ("short horizon") and a few "sparse concept" positions. Experiments on Qwen3-8B only (200 fitting examples): keeping only the top 10-20% highest-energy position pairs raised intermediate-concept recall from 18.71 to about 22 (average ICR over six tasks); removing the diagonal lowered both abilities; they could not decouple the two. The authors state the work is in progress.

### 1.8 Replications on open-weight models and serious critique

Found two third-party repositories. Neither is peer reviewed, both are READMEs only [README]; I did not inspect their code or rerun anything.
- alexanderquispe/jlens-open-models. Uses the Neuronpedia lenses (not Anthropic's own fits). Reported: intermediate-concept pass@10 J-lens 0.414 vs logit lens 0.277 on Llama-3.1-8B (gap in layers 15-23), similar sign on Llama-Instruct and OLMo-3-7B, but REVERSED on Qwen3-8B (0.246 vs 0.291), where the J-lens reads many intermediates in Chinese (0.304 vs 0.314 with Chinese targets). Ablating the J-space (k=10) in layers 4-19 cut two-hop recall to 0.60-0.73 on Llama versus 0.90-0.98 for random same-covariance directions; on Qwen the control also hurts recall (0.41-0.56 vs 0.75-0.88 in layers 12-23), so the specificity is weaker. J-lens swap of the intermediate country moves answers 76-90% on all three models; Qwen's logit lens is nearly as causal (0.50-0.73). Variance explained by J-space about 7-11% (k=10). INFERENCE: this is the nearest thing to a replication, and the Qwen result matters for me because E1 uses Qwen3.
- m9h/jacobian-lens (Hough, OREL). Reports an OLMo-3 post-training ladder, a "reviewer battery" of six tests proposed by Dehaene and Naccache (two clean signatures, one partial, three inconclusive "under first-pass adaptations whose flaws are documented"), cross-validation against Neuronpedia's lens (final layer cosine 0.9998 but 0.884 at layer 0), and two self-reported negatives: "Introspection came out as steering, not introspection", and the covert error signal does not improve best-of-N (+0.008). The introspection negative is a one-line README claim; I saw raw JSON in results/introspection but no write-up, so I cannot say how it was tested. Do not cite it as a result until the write-up is read.
- Anthropic's release includes a PDF "External commentary" from invited reviewers (www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/cc4be2488d65e54a6ed06492f8968398ddc18ebe.pdf). My fetch tool could not extract its text (compressed binary), so I could not read it. A search snippet quotes it calling the Jacobian-on-pretraining-data approach crude and noting that n=1,000 prompts makes replication expensive. That is a search snippet [web-summary], not a read.

### 1.9 Natural Language Autoencoders [web-summary]

transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/. Two LLM modules trained jointly with RL: an activation verbalizer writes text about a residual activation, an activation reconstructor maps the text back; objective is reconstruction (fraction of variance explained). Claude Haiku 3.5, Haiku 4.5, Opus 4.6. Trained NLAs reach 0.6-0.8 FVE from a warm start of about 0.3-0.4. No ground truth for model beliefs; validation by constructed inputs, steering, attribution graphs and an automated auditing benchmark where NLA-equipped agents beat baselines including SAE ones (scores not stated in the summary). Limits stated: explanations can contain false specifics, black box, expensive (hundreds of tokens per activation), objective could be gamed. Releases training code and NLAs for popular open models via Neuronpedia. Measures: verbalized content of activations, not what the model can report about itself. Relevance to me: low for E1 (no introspective-access claim; the summary says the paper does not analyze whether the model itself has access). Medium as an alternative reader of injected activations: an NLA can say what an injected vector "means" without asking the model, which separates "the activation carries X" from "the model reports X".

### 1.10 Emotion Concepts and their Function [web-summary of anthropic.com page; transformer-circuits page too large to fetch]

Claude Sonnet 4.5. 171 emotion words; stories written per emotion; activation difference gives an "emotion vector" per emotion. Causal steering: "desperate" up raises blackmail and reward-hacking, "calm" lowers them (blackmail baseline 22% in an earlier snapshot; the released model rarely does it). Vectors track the current-output-relevant emotion rather than a persistent state; inherited from pretraining, shifted by post-training. Authors say the findings do not show subjective experience. Relevance to me: concept vectors built as stories minus baseline are the same construction family as the Lindsey concept vectors E1 uses; the paper is evidence that such vectors causally steer behavior without any report step. Cheating driven by desperation sometimes showed no visible emotional markers in the output, which is a case of internal state affecting behavior without being verbalized.

## INFERENCE: implications for E1

### (a) Is the E1 question still open?

INFERENCE. In the pages I read, nobody has tested whether an injected position wins on tasks that do not mention injection. Gurnee et al. test concept identity under an injection-aware prompt and note the model seems to read J-space at user-prompt positions; the other applications are about monitoring, not about position localization. So the capture-versus-report question is open, but it is reframed in two ways:
1. The workspace account makes "report" and "capture" less exclusive than the prereg treats them. If an injected position holds readable J-space content, then "the model reports what it holds when asked" and "that position is salient" can both be true. E1's three outcomes still discriminate task-gating, but not whether the carrier is J-space.
2. The J-lens paper's own mechanism for detection (attending to J-space at user positions) is a salience-type route. That raises the prior for the prereg's "capture" outcome slightly and is consistent with the pilot showing L_pick above L_inj on a 0.6B model (a pilot, not evidence).
Caveat: I read about 15 of roughly 100 pages of 2607.15495; a localization test might sit in unread pages.

### (b) What the workspace account predicts for E1; does T_pick lift vs T_inj lift discriminate J-space?

INFERENCE.
- Predictions from the account: (i) the lift on T_inj should depend on the J-space component of the injected vector, not the non-J component (the swap experiment's logic); (ii) selectivity says automatic, low-demand tasks proceed without J-space, so a workspace-mediated effect should be task-gated (T_inj, T_fav, T_odd read the content; T_pick need not); (iii) a norm or attention-sink style salience effect, which needs no vocabulary content, would show up on T_pick regardless of J-space. (iv) The J-lens is a bag of concepts with no binding (their own limitation), so the J-space does not by itself say which position holds a concept; position binding must come from attention (compatible with Zou's head-level circuit).
- Does T_pick vs T_inj discriminate J-space? On its own, no. C_pick measures task-gating, which is orthogonal to whether the carried information is in J-space. A high C_pick says "capture" in either world; a low C_pick says "gated" in either world. It only discriminates when crossed with a J-space manipulation: if the T_inj lift tracks the J-space component and the T_pick lift tracks something else (norm, non-J component), the two effects come from different routes.
- Cautions for the E1 design: (1) The J-lens is noisy in roughly the first third of layers and clean in an intermediate band, while the prereg expects the selected (l, alpha) to be early-layer; a J-space reading of early-layer injections is the weakest case. (2) The concept vectors (story/prompt activation minus baseline) are expected to be mostly non-J-space (J-space is at most about 10% of variance), so they might work through a non-J channel. (3) Qwen is the model family where the open-weight J-lens advantage over the logit lens reversed in one third-party test; check lens validity on Qwen3-4B before any J-space claim.

### (c) Three cheapest informative J-lens experiments (CPU Colab; 4x RTX 3090 for Qwen3-4B)

All reuse the released lenses (Qwen3-4B lens file about 459 MB; I did not read its config, so first confirm which checkpoint and fit data it used). Reading a lens is one matrix multiply per layer. No fitting is needed unless the checkpoint does not match E1's chat model, in which case fitting costs d_model backward passes per prompt (about 100 prompts suffice per the README, 1,000 in the paper). I have not seen a wall-clock figure for fitting; measure on one prompt first.

1. **Validity gate on Qwen3-4B (precondition, not novel).** Recompute intermediate-concept recall (J-lens vs logit lens) and the multi-hop J-lens swap on Qwen3-4B using the evaluation sets in anthropics/jacobian-lens. Compute: single GPU, short; a smaller version (Qwen3-1.7B, lens exists) fits Colab CPU in float32, as E1's own CPU tier already does. Falsifier: J-lens recall or swap success no better than the logit lens or random same-covariance controls, as seen on Qwen3-8B in the Quispe README. Then use the logit lens or skip J-space analysis for Qwen. Duplicates published work in spirit (Gurnee on Claude; Quispe README on Qwen3-8B); it is new only at 4B.
2. **J-space decomposition of E1's own injection vectors, then a vector-family test (most informative).** Step A (CPU, analysis only): for each concept vector at the chosen layer, compute lens top tokens and the k=16 gradient-pursuit J-space component and its share of the vector's norm; correlate per-concept J-share with measured L_inj and L_pick from E1 data. Step B (4x3090): in the E1 harness, add vector conditions of equal norm and equal impact (JS-matched): full concept vector, J-component only, residual only, and a single J-lens vector for a named token. Falsifier: J-component-only reproduces less than about half of the full-vector L_inj while residual-only reproduces most, or J-lens vectors at matched impact are no better than random directions. Then J-space membership is not what makes position localizable; this supports the norm/impact explanation (H3). Opposite result supports a J-space route for T_inj; L_pick under the same conditions says whether capture uses it. Cost: Step A negligible; Step B is a few extra vector conditions at one (l, alpha), roughly the cost of an additional E1 large-tier stage. Duplication: the J-component vs non-J-component swap was done on Claude for concept report (web-summary figure 59% vs 5%), not for localization, not on tasks that do not mention injection, not on open weights.
3. **Necessity: J-space ablation at the injected position.** In the E1 harness, project out the top-k (k=10, as in the paper) J-space directions at the injected position over the workspace-band layers after the injection, and compare L_inj, L_pick, L_fav, L_odd against ablating random same-covariance directions of the same variance (the control used by the Quispe README and the paper). Compute: same order as one E1 stage per condition on the 4x3090. Falsifier: ablation leaves L_inj and L_pick unchanged relative to the random-direction control, so the lift is not routed through J-space. A drop in T_inj but not T_pick would be the clean dissociation described in (b). Duplication: ablation controls exist in the paper (Claude) and the Quispe README (two-hop recall, Llama/Qwen/OLMo); applied to injected-position selection it appears unpublished in what I found.

Caveat on all three: the J-lens needs the injection layer inside the workspace band to be interpretable, and the Qwen reversal means experiment 1 can legitimately stop the program. Pre-register the thresholds before running, as E1 does.
